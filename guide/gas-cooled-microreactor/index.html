Gas-cooled reactor (park)

Gas-cooled microreactor with salt storage: how it works

The gas-cooled reactor in the park circulates helium through TRISO fuel. Hot helium heats molten salt, and two tanks store the salt like a thermal battery, so the turbine can follow the data center while the reactor runs flat out.

Helium pressure vessel

This plant cools its core with helium gas under pressure. Helium does not react with anything and cannot boil. The vessel is a tall steel cylinder with the core in the middle.

CoolantHelium at ≈ 7 MPa
Core outlet≈ 630 °C
Thermal power≈ 15 MWt
Core life≈ 20 years

TRISO fuel

Each fuel kernel is smaller than a poppy seed and wears four ceramic coatings. The coatings hold in fission products to well above 1,600 °C. The display case shows one particle cut open at large scale.

KernelUranium oxycarbide, ≈ 0.5 mm
CoatingsBuffer, PyC, SiC, PyC
Particle size≈ 1 mm
Particles per reactorBillions

Helium circulator

A motor-driven blower on top of the vessel pushes helium down the outside of the core and up through the fuel. If it stops, the core cools itself by conduction and radiation.

TypeElectric, magnetic bearings
Flow≈ 10 kg/s
Power draw≈ 300 kW

Helium-to-salt heat exchanger

Hot helium hands its heat to a molten salt here. The salt then leaves the reactor building, so no nuclear system runs through the power plant next door.

Hot sideHelium, 630 °C in
Cold sideNitrate salt, 560 °C out
TypePrinted-circuit or shell-and-tube

Molten-salt storage tanks

Two tanks store hot and cold salt. The reactor runs flat out all day. The salt releases heat to the turbine when the data center needs it, like a thermal battery.

SaltSodium and potassium nitrate
Hot tank≈ 560 °C
Storage≈ 8 hours at full output
Tank size≈ 4.5 m dia × 8 m

Steam turbine-generator

Hot salt boils water in a steam generator, and the steam drives this turbine. It is ordinary power-plant equipment, and it can run faster or slower than the reactor because the tanks sit in between.

CycleSteam Rankine
Electric output≈ 5 MWe
Steam≈ 540 °C
CoolingDry air-cooled condenser

About

A representative design for education. Values are typical of a 10 MWe class pool-type sodium-cooled microreactor and of a data center of about 8 MW. They do not describe a specific product.
