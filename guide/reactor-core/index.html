Reactor core

Reactor core: fuel, reflector, control drums, shutdown rods

The core is where fission happens. It is small, about 1.2 meters across and 1 meter tall, and it makes about 30 megawatts of heat. These parts hold the fuel, reflect neutrons back in, set the power, and stop the reaction when needed.

Reactor core

The core is the compact region where fission takes place. Fuel assemblies sit in a tight hexagonal lattice, and the reflector surrounds them.

Thermal power≈ 30 MWt
Active height≈ 1.0 m
Diameter≈ 1.2 m
RefuelingWhole-core swap every 5–10 years

Fuel assemblies

Each assembly is a hexagonal steel duct that holds a bundle of fuel pins. Sodium flows upward through the duct and carries the heat away.

Count61
Duct materialHT-9 ferritic steel
Lattice pitch≈ 135 mm
Pins per assembly91, wire-wrapped

Fuel pins

Fuel pins are sealed steel tubes filled with uranium fuel pellets. A wire wrapped around each pin keeps the pins apart and lets sodium flow between them.

Fuel formU-Zr metal or UO₂, HALEU (< 20% U-235)
CladdingHT-9 steel
Pin diameter≈ 9 mm
Peak cladding temp.≈ 650 °C

Neutron reflector

The reflector ring surrounds the core and bounces escaping neutrons back in. It lets a small core sustain a chain reaction with less fuel.

MaterialBeryllium oxide or graphite in steel cans
Radial thickness≈ 280 mm
Height≈ 1.1 m
Also houses12 control drums

Control drums

Control drums are rotating cylinders inside the reflector. One face is a neutron absorber: turned toward the core it lowers power, turned away it raises power.

Count12
AbsorberBoron carbide (B₄C) sector, 120°
Diameter≈ 220 mm
DriveStepper motor, 0–180° rotation

Shutdown rods

Shutdown rods drop into the core to stop the chain reaction. Gravity inserts them when the holding magnets lose power, so the system needs no operator action.

Count3
AbsorberBoron carbide (B₄C)
Insertion time< 2 s
HoldElectromagnetic latch, fail-safe
