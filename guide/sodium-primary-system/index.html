Primary system

Sodium primary system: vessel, pool, pumps, heat exchangers

The primary system holds the core in a pool of liquid sodium at near atmospheric pressure. Pumps with no moving parts drive the sodium, heat exchangers hand the heat to a clean loop, and a second vessel catches any leak.

Reactor vessel

The reactor vessel is a stainless-steel tank that holds the core and the sodium pool. It operates near atmospheric pressure because sodium boils at 883 °C, far above operating temperature.

Material316H stainless steel
Height≈ 5.5 m
Diameter≈ 2.4 m
Design pressureNear atmospheric

Guard vessel

A second steel vessel surrounds the reactor vessel with a small gap. If the inner vessel leaks, the guard vessel catches the sodium and keeps the core covered.

Gap≈ 150 mm, argon filled
MaterialStainless steel
Leak detectionGap sensors and level probes
FunctionSecond barrier for sodium

Sodium coolant pool

Liquid sodium fills the vessel and carries heat from the core to the heat exchangers. It conducts heat very well and stays liquid from 98 °C to 883 °C.

Core inlet≈ 400 °C
Core outlet≈ 550 °C
Inventory≈ 20 tonnes
Cover gasArgon at ~1 atm

Electromagnetic pumps

EM pumps drive sodium with magnetic fields instead of impellers. They have no moving parts, no seals, and nothing to wear out.

Count2
TypeAnnular linear induction
Flow≈ 150 kg/s each
BackupNatural circulation

Intermediate heat exchangers

The IHX moves heat from the radioactive primary sodium to a clean intermediate loop. Thousands of thin tubes give the two fluids a large contact area without mixing.

Count2
TypeShell-and-tube, counter-flow
Duty≈ 15 MWt each
Secondary fluidSodium or molten salt

Reactor head & drives

The head closes the vessel and carries every penetration. Rod drives, instrument lines, and pipe nozzles pass through it, and a rotating plug gives access for refueling.

Deck thickness≈ 300 mm steel
Rotating plug≈ 1.8 m diameter
PenetrationsRod drives, IHX nozzles, instruments
Shield plugBorated steel and concrete below deck
