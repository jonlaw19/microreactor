Molten-salt reactor (park)

Molten-salt microreactor: how it works

The molten-salt reactor in the park dissolves its fuel in the coolant. If power fails, a frozen plug melts and the fuel drains by gravity into a tank where it cannot stay critical.

Molten-salt reactor vessel

In this plant the fuel is dissolved in the coolant. A hot salt with uranium in it flows through channels in a graphite block, where it goes critical, and then out to a heat exchanger.

Fuel saltFluoride salt with uranium
Temperature≈ 650 °C at near atmospheric pressure
Thermal power≈ 30 MWt
ModeratorGraphite

Fuel-salt pump

A pump on the vessel head moves the fuel salt around the loop. The motor sits above the salt, out of the heat, with a long shaft down to the impeller.

TypeVertical shaft, sump pump
Flow≈ 0.5 m³/s
Shaft sealInert gas

Primary heat exchanger

Fuel salt on one side, a clean coolant salt on the other. Fission products stay in the primary loop. The coolant salt carries the heat to the steam plant.

TypeShell-and-tube
MaterialNickel alloy
Secondary saltFLiBe or FLiNaK

Freeze plug and drain tank

A small blower keeps a plug of salt frozen in the drain line. If power fails, the blower stops, the plug melts, and the fuel drains by gravity into this tank, where it cannot stay critical and cools passively.

PlugFrozen salt, ≈ 20 mm pipe
Drain timeMinutes
TankFlat, subcritical shape with cooling fins

Off-gas system

Gaseous fission products bubble out of the liquid fuel. The off-gas system pulls them into charcoal beds, holds them while they decay, and keeps the loop clean.

GasesXenon, krypton, tritium
Hold-upCharcoal beds, days to weeks
CarrierHelium sweep gas

Steam plant

Coolant salt boils water in the steam generator, and the steam turns the turbine-generator under the canopy. The reactor cell wall sits between here and the fuel.

Electric output≈ 10 MWe
Steam≈ 550 °C
CoolingDry air-cooled condenser
